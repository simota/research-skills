# Traps — appraise

- **Peer review is a floor, not a ceiling.** It catches obvious defects; it does not verify data,
  guarantee replication, or make the finding apply to your case. Read the method regardless.
- **Prestige transfers badly across topics.** A world expert commenting outside their field is `P4`.
- **The date on the page is the publication date, not the evidence date.** Find the year of the
  benchmark, dataset, survey, or product version. That is the real date of the claim.
- **"Studies show" with no citation is `P5`, whoever wrote it.** So is a statistic whose trail ends
  at another article that also has no citation.
- **Sample size is not rigour.** A large biased sample is worse than a small representative one:
  it produces a precise wrong answer with narrow confidence intervals.
- **The reference list is the fastest quality test.** If its entries do not resolve, or do not say
  what they are cited for, nothing else about the source matters.
- **Confirming sources get graded loosely.** Deliberately grade the sources agreeing with your prior
  first and hardest — that is where the grading slips.
- **A missing comparison is a finding.** "40% faster" with no stated baseline, version, or workload
  is not a measurement; it is a marketing claim in numeric form.
