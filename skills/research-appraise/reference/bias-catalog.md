# Bias Catalog

Purpose: Naming interests, selection effects, and framing distortions.
Read when: naming interests, selection effects, and framing distortions
Source: none — nothing outside this page can move what it states.
Verified: 2026-08-21 — no automated check.

Bias is not dishonesty. Most of these operate on careful, sincere people — including the
researcher running this appraisal.

## In the source

| Bias | Mechanism | Detection | Handling |
|------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Interest alignment | Author benefits if the claim is believed | Who sells, funds, or competes | Cap at `P4` **for comparative and promotional claims**; documented own-product behaviour stays `P1`. Require independent corroboration |
| Funding | Sponsor's preferred outcome is the outcome | Funding statement; sponsor's product wins | Note; look for contrary-funded work |
| Selection | The sample was not drawn from the population being claimed about | Sample frame vs. claimed scope | Restrict the claim to the actual population |
| Survivorship | Failures were never in the dataset | Successes only; no abandoned cases | Actively search for failures; note publication asymmetry |
| Publication | Null and negative results go unpublished | Literature is uniformly positive | Discount consensus built from positive results only |
| Framing | Wording steers the conclusion | Leading questions, loaded terms | Re-read the raw data past the narrative |
| Recency | The latest development is treated as the trend | Trend claimed from one data point | Ask for the series |
| Anchoring on the first number | An early estimate propagates uncorrected | The same figure everywhere, one origin | Trace to origin (`research-source/reference/search-strategies.md` §Origin tracing) |
| Cherry-picked baseline | Comparison chosen after seeing results | Odd version, unusual config, unnamed baseline | Demand the baseline; otherwise unusable |
| Citation circularity | A repeats B repeats C repeats A | Follow every reference chain | Collapse to the primary; recount corroboration |

## In the corpus

- **Vendor-dominated**: every source has a commercial stake. State it; do not present the
  resulting consensus as independent.
- **Single-language / single-region**: the market's other half is invisible. Name the gap.
- **Search-authority skew**: well-optimised content outranks better-sourced content. Registry and
  primary channels correct for this.
- **Temporal clustering**: the whole corpus dates to one news cycle, all downstream of one event.
- **Community monoculture**: one forum, one conference, one company's ex-employees — shared priors
  look like agreement.

## In the researcher

Name these explicitly; the prior recorded by `research-scope` exists for this.

| Bias | Manifestation here | Counter |
|------|--------------------|---------|
| Confirmation | Confirming sources graded loosely, contradicting ones interrogated hard | Grade confirming sources first and hardest |
| Desirability | The convenient answer needs less evidence | Ask what evidence a skeptic would demand |
| Effort justification | A hard-won source is over-weighted | Grade by method only; retrieval cost is irrelevant |
| Premature closure | Search stops at the first coherent answer | Honour the disconfirming query before concluding |
| Narrative pull | Contradictions smoothed into a clean story | Contradictions are findings; carry them forward |

## Handling rules

1. Name the interest as a fact, without moral framing: "vendor-authored", not "untrustworthy".
2. Interest sets a **ceiling**, not a veto. The only measurement that exists is still the only one.
3. Two interest-aligned sources on the same side are one source for confidence purposes.
4. A source that discloses its own funding and limitations earns a small upgrade in trust; one that
   conceals a discoverable interest earns a downgrade and a note.
5. For empirical/comparative/promotional claims, when every source is interest-aligned,
   say so and record `CEILING: Low` for synthesis —
   that state of the evidence is itself the most useful thing to report. Recommend the ceiling;
   do not assign the label.
