# Traps — synthesize

- **Silence is not agreement.** A source that does not discuss a claim is not supporting it. The
  matrix needs a "silent" state, or every claim looks unanimously supported.
- **Consensus is usually one source wearing five hats.** Trace agreement to origins before calling
  it consensus. This single check overturns more conclusions than any other.
- **The strongest source may be the outlier.** When one rigorous study contradicts many weak ones,
  the weight of evidence favours the outlier. Counting says otherwise; counting is wrong.
- **Definitional disputes look like factual ones.** "Is X faster than Y" often means the two camps
  measure different things. Diagnose before adjudicating.
- **Absence of evidence has two meanings.** Nobody looked, or people looked and found nothing.
  Those license very different conclusions — distinguish them explicitly.
- **A tidy synthesis is a warning sign.** Real evidence is ragged. If everything resolved cleanly,
  check whether the disconfirming search ever ran.
- **Inference is where fabrication enters.** The chain "A is true, and A usually implies B, so B"
  produces a claim with no source. It may still be right — but it is labelled inference or it is
  not in the ledger.
